Draft for Mary · pitch

The 75-second pitch

Draft Written from the team's research. Mary decides what goes into the final story.

Basel wants rain to stay where it falls: held, soaked up, and breathed back out by trees. So why is one rain garden so hard?

Because a rain garden is not a hole with plants. Under a metre of pavement there are roots, cables, water mains, district heating, old fill and groundwater. Above it there are trams, deliveries and people. Half a dozen offices and operators each hold one piece of the answer. The Tiefbauamt asks whether the street still works. IWB asks whether the networks stay safe. The AUE asks whether water may enter this ground. And the Stadtgärtnerei asks the question that decides whether the whole thing is still alive in twenty years: can it live here?

That is not bureaucracy failing. It is a city protecting what it cannot see.

Basel Street X-Ray makes that hidden collaboration visible. Pick a street. See what public data shows, what can be derived, and what is restricted or must be checked on site. See who owns each open question. Leave with a Street Evidence Passport: a first brief that every office can read.

We don't ask a map to approve a rain garden. We give Basel a better first conversation, not a permit.
